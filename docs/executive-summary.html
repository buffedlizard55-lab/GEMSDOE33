Why did GEMSDOE32 B2 score 0.2778 — and can we beat it?

We reproduced B2 pixel-exactly from the GEMSDOE28 H27-4 owner raster: it deletes 2,545 dots within 2 px of the catalogue and adds nothing (40,199 → 37,654 dots; Spearman ρ = 0.9676 vs H27-4; 100% of B2 dots within 3 px of H27-4 dots). Under the competition DTI (α=0.2 per false positive, β=0.8 per missed truth, 300 m kernel), every isolated prediction dot costs roughly 1 unit of weighted false-positive mass unless it lies within 300 m of a truth pixel, while dense clusters of dots share the same truth credit. Removing low-credit, catalogue-adjacent dots can therefore raise DTI even though no new fault is found: the most plausible mechanism is FP-mass reduction, not discovery. That is an inference from audited bytes, not proof, and no organizer receipt ties 0.2778 to B2's exact bytes.

Can we beat it? Not demonstrated here. Every fold-safe surface in this repository reaches HOLDOUT-DTI 0.13–0.14. The newest, A33-R3-LLC, tied CSHT: Δ +0.0003 on the screen and −0.0006 on confirmation, with both CIs spanning 0. The fixed H19-5 surface that B2 descends from is not fold-safe, and on the same withheld segments it reaches 0.162. That gap of +0.0242 [+0.0150, +0.0328] (4/4 folds) is either real signal or leakage. That experiment has now run (FSML/FSML2-CC, 2026-10-26): the fold-safe re-derivation with the cached channel stack did not reproduce the gap — the plain PU blend duplicated CSHT at the surface guard (87.5% 3-px overlap), and the unique corroboration-count variant scored HOLDOUT-DTI 0.1104/0.1031 vs CSHT 0.1357/0.1321. So H19-5’s advantage either uses information outside the cached stack (1 m DEM tiles, full-catalogue geometry) or is not fold-safe; it is not reproducible as a unique fold-safe lane here.
