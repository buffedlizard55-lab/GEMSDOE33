Mission and current standing

The DOE GEMS Prize Challenge asks for fault predictions in the GeoDAWN region of the northwestern Great Basin. The prior best, 0.2708 for h27-4-r1-solo-d2-8, is an owner-reported manual result, not an organizer receipt archived here. This score belongs specifically to h27-4-r1-solo-d2-8; the separate GEMSDOE28 H36-1 and H38-1 experiments remain unscored and are not the source of 0.2708. The official public leaderboard snapshot stored here (checked 2026-10-03 local) lists nchuzhoy at 0.3262; it is dated and may change. No GEMSDOE33 file has a competition score.

No new candidate is cleared for a submission slot. The earlier C2 P1 PASS was withdrawn after review found that the hidden catalogue systems remained in C2's Qfaults source features and the C0/C2 construction used the full catalogue.

Why the owner-reported 0.2708 did well

  • Metric: distance-weighted Tversky index (α=0.2, β=0.8) with a 300 m triangular credit kernel. The owner-reported score sequence indicates that precision/recall balance and location quality mattered more than drawing continuous fault traces.
  • Construction: the h27-4 raster is reported as 40,199 sparse dots based on the H19-5 scarp/geophysics ridge ensemble, Poisson-thinned to 2.8 px, with a blind one-pixel catalogue-flank prune. Pixels near known catalogue faults do not explain the off-catalogue target by themselves.
  • What the next score requires: new, independent off-catalogue information plus conservative error control. A previous numerical budget calculation was tied to the old 0.3195 leaderboard value; it is stale against the 0.3262 snapshot. Recompute it from the exact baseline counts before quoting any required recall, dot count, or probability of winning. No live-score gain is projected from a proxy.

The reported 0.2708 explains why a sparse, catalogue-aware baseline is valuable; it does not prove each removal is a false positive or guarantee that further thinning will help. See the full metric and error-budget analysis.

Candidate science: what is tested and what is not

C2 is implemented as a research hypothesis, not a promoted candidate. It proposes short, sparse bridges between tips of distinct mapped fault features where band 18, iso_grav_anom_hg, is high. The rule uses a 300–2,500 m tip gap, ≤30° strike difference, and band-18 threshold; the full-data raster adds 940 dots. The original P1 test was invalid because hidden Qfaults systems and full-catalogue masks remained in construction. This is a structural hypothesis, not proof that any candidate bridge is a hidden fault. The literature citation (Faulds & Hinz, 2015, regional Great Basin structural-settings study) provides regional structural context. It is not the NBMG OF 03-27 map report; the official NBMG product is linked separately in data sources.

H33-A analog transfer is a design only, not a validated result. Dixie Valley / Desert Peak / Brady source maps must first be geolocated and their fault/thermal labels audited. The GDR #207 page lists Dixie Valley files, but the workspace request for GIS_Faults.zip failed at TLS/connection setup; no archive bytes were obtained. The set of usable features must be established from the actual spatial overlap and equivalent processing; do not assume that analog products contain or match the GeoDAWN 19-band raster. GDR #1288 supplies geothermal-feature and classification material, not GeoDAWN truth labels or an equivalent 19-band raster. Transfer cannot be claimed without label-definition compatibility, CRS/resolution/coverage checks, shared-feature construction, source holdouts, and target-side proxy validation.

A formal adaptation bound is a useful warning, not a shortcut: εT(h) ≤ εS(h) + ½ dHΔH(DS, DT) + λ, where λ is the joint error of the best shared hypothesis. A classifier-two-sample test can estimate a divergence term only for a pre-defined common representation; it does not establish a small λ, compatible labels, or reliable target predictions. The register ranks H33-A and four alternatives by expected value/cost while clearly marking unknowns.

H33-B is also unrun. The 2 m temperature-probe archive is listed on GDR #1391 but is not in the current restored candidate cache. Its residual surface would need its own background model and sampling bias checks. USGS conductive heat-flow and well-residual products are distinct evidence, not the same thing as 2 m probe temperature. Band 5 is iso_grav_anom_slope, a gravity derivative; it is not a thermal feature.

Validation discipline and corrected conditional diagnostic

Leakage review: the initial P1 runner hid labels at scoring time but left full-catalogue geometry in candidate construction and Qfaults source vectors. Its apparent C2 PASS is withdrawn; the earlier C1–C5 P1 values are not valid promotion evidence. The old report is preserved with a withdrawal annotation.

  • Corrected P1: four spatial folds; rebuild the H27-4 base from each fold-visible catalogue, exclude Qfaults source IDs within the 600 m holdout buffer plus 100 m raster guard, and use the masked catalogue for C2 construction.
  • P2: SGMC off-catalogue faults at least 300 m from the supplied catalogue; a separate compilation proxy, not organizer truth.
  • Numeric gate: P1 mean ΔDTI > 0, a strict majority of folds positive, and P2 ΔDTI ≥ −0.001. C2 fails because corrected P1 is negative in every fold.
  • Remaining caveat: the legacy H19-5 ridge raster is held fixed rather than re-derived per fold; its upstream source/training code is not available here. This is a conditional diagnostic, not an independent confirmation.

Limitations and next decision gates

  • H33-A: fetch/inspect the official source data, geolocate and normalize candidate fault layers, establish label semantics, construct genuinely common features, and run spatial/source validation. Do not treat GDR geothermal-feature files as the GeoDAWN 19-band training raster.
  • H33-B: restore and audit the 2 m probe archive listed on GDR #1391; distinguish shallow probe anomalies from the USGS heat-flow residual products.
  • H33-D: reconcile GEMSDOE29's radiometric record before calling the idea novel or running it.
  • Submission: no candidate currently passes the source-excluded P1 gate. Do not use a slot until fold-safe P1/P2 evidence and exact format checks pass. The official rules allow three submissions per week but require a single final selection across both prize phases, and require a narrative disclosure of generative-AI use. The draft disclosure in the upload guide must be reviewed by the owner.
  • Access: the official Terms of Use restrict automated monitoring and manual copying without written consent; the leaderboard feed remains static and date-stamped. No code logs in, uploads, scrapes, or polls DrivenData.
  • Reproducibility: the local cache may need restoration with bash scripts/download_competition_data.sh; restore code pins/checks public mirrors and never fetches competition submissions from DrivenData.
