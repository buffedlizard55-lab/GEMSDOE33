Why the owner-reported 0.2708 file did well

The competition describes the hidden target as expert-labelled faults missing from existing public catalogues, scored with a distance-weighted Tversky index (α=0.2, β=0.8, 300 m triangular kernel). Owner-reported campaign history points to three compounding effects:

  1. Dotting beats solid lines: sparse dots spaced at most 3 pixels apart keep useful kernel credit on fault midpoints while limiting false-positive mass (reported progression 0.1922 → 0.2477 → 0.2600).
  2. Off-catalogue placement: predictions on or near known catalogue traces add denominator mass but do not identify hidden faults.
  3. Blind flank removal: removing a one-pixel catalogue flank reportedly raised the score by +0.0108. This is owner-reported; it is not a guarantee that all such pixels are false positives.

The gap to the current public leader is a ranking/detection problem that needs independent information. C2 tests a bounded stepover-bridge hypothesis with two local proxies. The separate H33-A analog-field transfer is still unvalidated: it needs geolocation, label/feature compatibility, common-layer construction, and blocked holdouts. GDR geothermal-feature layers are not the competition's 19-band training raster.
