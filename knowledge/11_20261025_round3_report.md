### 1.2 Mathematical Mechanism Behind `0.2778` & Why the `[0, 1]` Portal Error Occurred

1. **Submodular Coverage vs. Additive False-Positive Penalty ($121\text{k} \to 60\text{k} \to 44\text{k}$ dots):**
   Under the official Distance-Weighted Tversky Index ($\alpha = 0.2, \beta = 0.8$, kernel $k(d) = \max(0, 1 - d/3\text{ px})$) scored on $A = \text{Footprint} \setminus C_{\text{mapped}}$, $\text{TP}_w(P) = \sum_{g \in G_{\text{new}}} \max_{x \in P} k(d(x, g))$ uses a **pointwise maximum** over predicted dots, whereas $\text{FP}_w(P) = \sum_{x \in P} [1 - \max_{g \in G_{\text{new}}} k(d(x, g))]$ sums **additively** over every emitted dot. Contiguous 100 m lines waste >60% of their dot budget inside already-covered 300 m cones; spacing dots by $\approx 2.6\text{–}2.8\text{ px}$ eliminates redundant FP mass while preserving >90% of tube coverage.
2. **The Mapped-Catalogue Blur-Halo Effect ($d_{\text{cat}} \le 2.0\text{ px}$, `44,090 -> 40,199 -> 37,654` dots):**
   Potential-field and 100 m DEM gradient detectors blur across $\pm 200\text{ m}$ ($2\text{ px}$) on both sides of **already-mapped** faults $C_{\text{mapped}}$. Because the scorer masks $C_{\text{mapped}}$ pixel-exactly, flanking dots at $0 < d_{\text{cat}} \le 2.0\text{ px}$ are **not** masked out — they are scored as active off-catalogue predictions even though their physical source is the *already-mapped* fault rather than a new fault $G_{\text{new}}$. At $\text{DTI} = S \approx 0.271$, a dot improves DTI iff its marginal $\Delta\text{TP}_w / \Delta\text{FP}_w > \frac{\alpha S}{1 - \alpha S} \approx 0.0573$. Removing the `3,891` $d_{\text{cat}} \le 1\text{ px}$ flank dots (`GEMSDOE28`) and the `2,545` $1 < d_{\text{cat}} \le 2\text{ px}$ flank dots (`GEMSDOE32`) stripped `6,436` mapped-fault halo false positives without sacrificing off-catalogue fault coverage.
3. **Why `-zeros.tif` Resolves the `[0, 1]` Portal Range Rejection:**
   Line-by-line inspection of the official DrivenData U-Net reference notebook (`drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution`, Cell 19) confirms the organizer's own export template initializes `y_out = np.zeros(...)` and writes `predicted_faults_example.tif` with `0.0` outside valid patches and `nodata=None`. In IEEE-754 floating-point arithmetic, `NaN >= 0.0` evaluates to `False`; an unmasked whole-raster check `(arr >= 0) & (arr <= 1)` rejects `-nan.tif` (`7,111,787` outside-footprint NaNs) with `"Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]"` while accepting all-finite `-zeros.tif` (`0.0` outside footprint, zero contribution to `TP_w` or `FP_w`).
4. **Can We Score Higher Than `0.2778`? Why `H19-5` Pruning Plateaus:**
   Every raster in the `0.2477 -> 0.2600 -> 0.2708 -> 0.2778` chain is a subset of the fixed heuristic `H19-5` ridge network. Once the $200\text{ m}$ mapped-fault blur halo is removed, `H19-5` cannot gain substantially further because its hand-crafted surface misses blind intrabasin and hydrothermal faults (`SGMC` off-catalogue `PROXY-DTI = 0.0961`). Breaking above the `H19-5` plateau requires **replacing the underlying detection surface** with a calibrated multi-physics posterior + exact submodular 300 m Tversky allocation — while recognizing that **no local proxy can guarantee a live leaderboard score without an organizer submission receipt**.
