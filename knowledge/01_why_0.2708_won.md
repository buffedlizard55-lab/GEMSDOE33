## 1. The metric reduced to one line

DTI = TP / (TP + 0.2·FP + 0.8·FN) = TP / (0.2·TP + 0.2·FP + 0.8·|G|), with a 300 m
triangular kernel on all three terms and |G| ≈ 12,226 hidden-truth pixels
(sibling-lattice calibration). False negatives are capped by |G|; false positives are
unbounded and — at ~40k emitted pixels — dominate the denominator (~49% of it).

**Inclusion rule:** adding pixels raises DTI iff dTP/dFP > 0.2·DTI/(1 − 0.2·DTI).
Break-even credit per unit FP mass: 0.052 at DTI 0.2477, **0.0573 at 0.2708**, 0.068 at 0.3195.

Three compounding effects explain 0.2708:

1. **Dotting beats solid lines.** For a 1-D fault crest, dots spaced s ≤ 3 px apart still
   give every intermediate truth pixel kernel credit ≥ 0.5 at the midpoint while each dot
   costs only 1 FP unit. d=2.8 retains 90.63% of the d=1.5 credit with 26.6% fewer dots —
   better than the 2-D isotropic retention model predicts, because the hidden truth is a
   1-D curvilinear network colinear with the ridge crest, not a 2-D sheet.
2. **The scoring truth is OFF-catalogue.** The test set is expert-labelled faults *not* in
   the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. Dots hugging known catalogue traces are FP mass that can
   never earn credit. In d=2.8, 3,891 dots (8.83%) sat exactly 100 m beside masked known
   faults (100–400 m USGS-vs-LiDAR scarp offsets, Hermant et al. 2025).
3. **Blind flank removal (H27-4).** Pruning the 2,434–3,891 mid-segment flank-shadow dots
   — while the prune is *blind* to any local truth signal, hence "blind" — cut pure
   denominator mass. Held-out evidence: mid-segment flank dots carry 0.0040 credit/FP,
   13.7× below the live break-even; removing them gained +0.0108 live.

## 3. What 0.3195 requires

Under the corrected naive closure (GEMSDOE28 knowledge/01 addendum), at the current
credit fraction (a ≈ 0.386 of |G|), 0.3195 needs N ≈ 24,970 dots instead of 44,090 —
the same credited mass in roughly half the dots — or, at the current dot count, credit
must rise to ~0.479 of hidden truth (+24%). Both are **ranking/detection problems**:
re-spacing the same ridge family cannot supply them (the d=1.5→2.8 axis is exhausted;
its marginal return 0.031 credit/removed dot is below the 0.055 break-even).
